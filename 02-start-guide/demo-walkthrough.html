Step 1

Before you start

Check these three things. If any one fails, follow start-guide.html first.

  1. Docker Desktop is open and says Engine running.
  2. http://localhost:5678 opens n8n and shows five workflows marked Published.
  3. The laptop is plugged in and will not go to sleep.
What is this project? Customer Voice Hub helps an after-sales quality team act on customer complaints. It reads each complaint (English, Hindi or Hinglish), finds faults that many customers report, ranks open service cases by risk, and prepares the follow-up: an email to the right team, a meeting, a leadership summary or a reply to the customer. All data is made up for the demo.
Step 2

Set up Claude

  1. Open the Claude desktop app and click New task (Cowork).
  2. Below the message box, open the tools / connectors menu and turn these on:
    Turn onWhy it is needed

    Hyundai Review IntelligenceScores a single complaint
    Hyundai Escalation RadarRanks open tickets and finds repeated faults
    GmailSaves escalation emails and briefs as drafts (never sends)
    Google CalendarBooks review meetings
    Google DriveHolds the data sheets and can store briefs
  3. The first time Claude uses a tool it may ask for permission. Click Allow.
Start a new task for each test below. Answers will be worded differently each time, but the numbers, teams and decisions should match the samples.

Step 4

Questions and answers

For anyone trying to understand the project.

What problem does it solve?

The same car fault is often reported by many customers in different cities, but each service advisor only sees one car. This system connects those reports, so a repeated fault like the gearbox judder (16 reports, 8 cities) is spotted early and sent to the right team.

Who would use it?

Customer Care executives check and route complaints. Quality managers decide what to escalate. Quality engineers receive escalation emails with evidence. Leadership reads the weekly brief and the website.

How much time does it save?

One complaint goes from about 30–45 minutes of manual checking, emailing and meeting setup to about 3 minutes, and the link to a repeated fault is no longer missed.

Where does the AI come in?

Google Gemini translates Hindi and Hinglish. Claude reads the tool results, applies the company rules (skills), explains its decisions and writes emails, briefs and replies. The n8n chatbot uses Gemini to decide which tool to call.

Why use rules as well as AI?

Risk and safety decisions must be consistent and explainable. The rules give the same answer every time and show their reasons. The AI adds judgment and writing, and it shows whenever it overrides a rule.

Where does the data come from?

Two Google Sheets: 86 service tickets and 180 customer reviews. They are made up for this demo. In a real company, the ticket sheet would be replaced by the dealer or CRM system.

What if the AI service is busy or down?

The translation step continues without AI so scoring still works. The chatbot retries automatically and switches to a backup AI model.

Does it send emails by itself?

No. It only creates drafts. A person always reviews and sends.

What does it cost to run?

Nothing for this demo: n8n runs free on the laptop, ngrok and Google Gemini are on free plans.

Step 7

Behind the scenes (optional)

To see how the data flowed for any answer above.

  1. Open n8n → Tool – Escalation Radar → Executions tab.
  2. Click the newest run. Click each step from left to right: the question that came in, the Google Sheet download, the 86 rows, and the ranked result.
  3. Click Debug in editor to see the number of items on each connecting line.
  4. Do the same in Tool – Score Review to see the Hinglish text, Gemini's English version and the score.
Try a live change: add a new open, safety-related Venue ticket to the Service Tickets sheet, wait about 5 minutes, then ask the chatbot "How many open tickets and how many to escalate today?" again. The numbers go up by one. (The website keeps its saved snapshot.)
